Section 03 — La Storia

A bit of
Italy.

How Tony & Marina Horani turned a small space on Jefferson Boulevard into Fort Wayne's most beloved café — and the only place in Indiana pulling Danesi espresso.

001 — The Founders

Tony & Marina Horani.

Marina grew up in Italy, where coffee isn't a transaction — it's a pause in the day. Tony, the host of the family. Together they decided Fort Wayne deserved a real Italian café.

When you walk in, one of them is at the counter. They'll know your face by the second visit. By the third, your drink. By the fifth, your kid's name. That's the entire idea.

002 — How We Got Here

From Rome.
To West Jefferson.

— Chapter One —

Italy.

Marina's family ran a small bakery in central Italy. Espresso was the first ritual of the morning, the punctuation between courses, the toast at the end of the night. Coffee wasn't a habit — it was a way of being together.

— Chapter Two —

America.

They moved to the Midwest, raised a family, ran kitchens. But every drip-machine cup of coffee was a small disappointment. They started talking about how it should be, and one day stopped just talking.

— Chapter Three —

The Beans.

They went hunting for an Italian roaster they could trust. Danesi — a Roman family business since 1905 — had never been served in Indiana. Tony & Marina would be the first.

— Chapter Four —

The Café.

They found a small space on Jefferson Boulevard. Painted it warm, hung Italian art, installed a traditional espresso machine, fired up a waffle iron, and opened the door. The line on the second day told them they'd done it right.

003 — How We Run It

Six things
we don't compromise on.

/01

Real espresso.

Pulled fresh, every cup. No automatic super-machines. No pre-ground hoppers sitting all morning.

/02

Real ingredients.

Whole milk that tastes like milk. Italian flour. Belgian sugar. Things you can pronounce.

/03

Real time.

A waffle takes four minutes. A pour-over takes five. We won't rush the things that matter.

/04

Real welcome.

We greet every person who walks in. By name when we know it. By eye contact when we don't.

/05

Local ties.

Pastries from a Naples-trained baker. Granola from a Fort Wayne kitchen. Honey from down the road.

/06

Reasonable prices.

A great cappuccino shouldn't cost $9. We price like Italians — for everyday, not for tourists.

"We didn't want a coffee shop.
We wanted a place —
the kind that exists on every corner in Italy.
A second living room."

— Tony Horani, Co-founder
